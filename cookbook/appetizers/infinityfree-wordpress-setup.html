InfinityFree/WordPress Setup Tutorial

The Appetizer to CMS Week

PART 1

Creating an InfinityFree Account

Your Hosting Provider for the Course

  1. In order to create a web site on the Internet, you first need to find someone/something to host it for you.

    The hosting provider we're going to be using for this first week of CMS is called InfinityFree.

    As their name implies, they are free! However, as with many free sites, there are a TON of ads. In particular, ads that look like buttons to click on, to trick you into visiting another site.

    So be sure to pay close attention to the screenshots we provide, and close any popups that show up that don't match!


    Start by visiting the following link:

    https://www.infinityfree.com/

    Then, click either of the purple [Register] or [Create Free Account] buttons. Both will take you to the same place

  2. On the next page, choose [Sign up with Email].

    Then, on the following form, enter your school email address, along with a new password.

    Check the box next to "I've read and agree to the terms of service".

    We always encourage you to actually read the terms of service, but for a short summary: don't use the site for anything sketchy.

    Make sure the Captcha check is green, and then click the purple [Sign up] button.

  3. Next, InfinityFree will send a confirmation email to the email address you specified.

    Open your Inbox to that email address, and once the email arrives, click on the [Verify Email Address] button inside.

  4. By doing so, you will have completed the InfinityFree account setup and find yourself on the Hosting Accounts page.

    Note the large ad at the top of the screenshot that says "PDF" along with tempting CTAs like "View Template (PDF)" and "Open"?

    IGNORE THEM! They have nothing to do with what we're setting up, and if you can close the ads that pop up on the screen, even better.

    You're now done with the first part of the setup process.

Congrats! You've successfully created your InfinityFree account! Now we can begin creating your very own site on InfinityFree.

PART 2

Creating an InfinityFree Hosting Account

Where WordPress will be built

  1. Now that you have your InfinityFree account setup, we can create an InfinityFree hosting account.

    Yes, we're aware how confusing that is. But that is the terminology InfinityFree uses: first you create an account with them, and then inside of that, you create a HOSTING account. Think of it as creating the website we're going to install WordPress on.

    Click the purple [+ Create Account] button to get started.

  2. Once you're on the 'Choose Hosting Plan' page, find the $0 option, and click the purple [Create Now] button at the bottom of it.

    Again, IGNORE ANY BUTTONS YOU SEE IN ADS!

  3. On the next page, you'll be asked to provide a subdomain (basically, just a name).

    We recommend using your first name and last name to make it easier for your instructor to grade in the next couple weeks.


    After typing in your subdomain name, click the [Check Availability] button.

    If your subdomain name is taken, come up with another one.

  4. Assuming your subdomain was available, you'll be brought to a new page.

    On this page, you are provided with a pre-generated username and password. You cannot change the username, but we do recommend changing (and saving!) the password.

    We also recommend picking the "I Do Not Approve" option under "Email Consent" so you don't get spam emails, but that is up to you.

    Once you've finished all that, click the purple [+ Create Account] button.

  5. After that, you will need to wait a few minutes for InfinityFree to set up your Hosting Account.

    Keep an eye on the section at the bottom of the page; while the process is running, you'll see a blue spinny circle.

    Once the process is complete, that section at the bottom of the page will turn green with a new button in it.

    Click the green [-> Go to Your Account] button.

  6. You should now be on the dashboard for your newly created InfinityFree Hosting Account.

    This is going to get confusing later if you don't pay attention, because we're also going to have a WordPress Dashboard.

    You have now completed the second part of the setup process.

Congrats! You have now created a InfinityFree Hosting Account and are ready to install WordPress on it!

PART 3

Installing WordPress

The CMS on your InfinityFree site

  1. By now, you should be on your Pantheon Site Dashboard.

    If you aren't, this can be found by clicking on the Sites tab in the far left navigation bar, and then clicking on the site name you want to access the dashboard for.

    Once on the Pantheon Site Dashboard for the site you want to work with, click on the [Dev Admin] button underneath the Dev tab, underneath the name of your site.

  2. You should be taken to the WordPress Install Flow.

    You may instead be brought to a 404 page, with a little cartoon Zeus character. If this happens, just wait a minute or two and refresh the page.

    This just means you were a little too quick, and there was a little more setup that needed to be done behind the scenes before you can start installing WordPress.

    Leave the language as "English (United States)", and click the blue [Continue] button.

  3. Next, you'll be brought to a form to fill out some information for the WordPress. Most of it is unimportant and can be changed later... EXCEPT FOR THE USERNAME AND PASSWORD.

    If you forget your username and password, the usual "Reset my password" link will NOT work with Pantheon's version of WordPress.

    There is a way to reset it, but it involves diving deep into the database and modifying the data inside, which is a complex and somewhat risky process.

    So do yourself a favor and WRITE DOWN YOUR USERNAME AND PASSWORD!

    We also recommend using the same username and password when you set up a second WordPress site.

    As for the Site Title, fill out the text field based on the instructions in your assignment.

    If you've forgotten what to fill out here, go back to the assignment!

    Your Email is not actually used for anything, so it doesn't matter what you put there.

    Leave the Search Engine Visibility checkbox unchecked.

    Finally, click the [Install Wordpress] button when you're done.

  4. Next, you should be brought to a "Success!" page, which basically just confirms your chosen Username.

    Click the "Log In" link to log into WordPress with the credentials you just specified.

  5. After logging in, you'll find yourself on your WordPress Dashboard.

    This dashboard is different from your Pantheon Site Dashboard. Don't get them confused!

    This is the Dashboard you'll be working in for most of the CMS portion of the course. From here, you can modify the site settings, add content, and change the site's appearance.

    The WordPress Dashboard may feel a bit overwhelming at first, but you'll get more comfortable with it over the course of the week.

    However, this is only the "backend" of your WordPress site, where things take place behind-the-scenes.

    Only you can access this dashboard; if you try to share links to this page, people won't be able to get in.

    To access the "frontend" of the WordPress site (the live version that other people can see), click on the little home icon at the top left of the Dashboard, next to the name of your site.

  6. After clicking the home icon, you'll be brought to the Home page of your WordPress site.

    This is actually your WordPress site, but at the moment, there's still a lot of placeholder content, such as "Mindblown: a blog about philosophy" in the screenshot to the right; this content is set using the default WordPress Theme, which changes every year.

    Don't worry, as you'll see soon, we can customize all of this! For now, just ignore the default Theme content.

    Notice how the home icon in the top left changed into a little speedometer icon instead. Clicking on this icon will bring you back to the Dashboard.

    That icon can be helpful in letting you know whether you can share a page with someone else.

    • If it's a speedometer icon (indicating you're NOT in the Dashboard), then you can share that URL with other people.
    • If it's a home icon (indicating you ARE on the Dashboard), then you can't share that URL with other people, since they won't be able to access it.

Congrats! You have now created a WordPress site! Go into the Dashboard and start customizing your site!
